What the job titles have in common

Product operations, healthcare data entry, business operations leadership, event management — on paper these look like four different careers but in practice it's been one job worn four ways: find where information or process has gone unreliable, and build the structure that fixes it — a reconciled dashboard, a documented SOP, a training session, a cleaner CRM pipeline.

A good amount of that work is, plainly, administrative — keeping records straight, handling client and vendor queries, running the paperwork behind a launch or a shift handover. I don't treat that as separate from the "data" work; the same attention to detail runs through both, which is why I'm equally at home leading an analysis or carrying the admin, scheduling, and customer-support load that keeps a remote team moving day to day.

I work async, across time zones, on whatever system a team already has — and lately that includes using AI tools like Claude and ChatGPT to move faster through code reviews, documentation, and research, with a hard rule against ever putting client or confidential data into them.
